# Exit & Resale — turning the flip into cash

The flip is only realized at exit. This phase is where amateurs leave 30–50% of the multiple on
the table. Package it like a pro.

## When to sell — timing signals
List when you can show a **clean, defensible trailing story**, not at the peak of a fragile spike:
- **3+ months of stable-or-growing** verified MRR since your changes (buyers price the trend).
- Churn brought down and **proven** with cohorts.
- **Owner-dependency removed** — documented SOPs, automations in place, you are no longer "the product."
- Any concentration risk reduced; key contracts renewed/locked.
- Pricing migration complete (no LTD overhang, recurring revenue clean).

Holding longer only helps if the trend keeps improving; once growth flattens and you've harvested
the easy levers, **sell and recycle the capital** into the next asset.

## Where to list

| Channel | When to use |
|---------|-------------|
| **Microns.io** | Micro-exits <$100k — fast, zero-commission, developer buyers. |
| **Acquire.com** | The default for most flips; 500k+ buyers, Stripe/GA verification builds trust. |
| **Flippa** | Broadest reach, more tire-kickers — fine for smaller/pre-revenue. |
| **Broker (e.g. FE International)** | Larger deals (mid-six-figures+) where a broker's buyer network and deal-running earns its fee. |
| **Direct / inbound** | If you've built in public and have an audience, a direct sale skips fees entirely. |

## Maximize the multiple before you list
- **Clean financials** — verified Stripe export, P&L, clear add-backs / SDE.
- **Recurring, not LTD** — card-on-file MRR reads far better than lifetime deals.
- **Documented ops** — the SOP doc is the single biggest multiple-mover; it turns "a job" into "an asset."
- **The before/after story** — show the delta you created (TTV, churn, MRR, traffic).
- **De-risk the transfer** — clean repo, transferable accounts, no personal API keys hardcoded.

## The data room / prospectus
Assemble (see `templates/exit-prospectus.md`):
1. One-line pitch + the value-creation story (the delta).
2. Verified metrics: MRR/ARR, growth, churn cohorts, NRR, traffic, unit economics.
3. P&L + SDE with add-backs explained.
4. Tech stack, dependencies, infra, and transfer plan.
5. The **SOP document** — how the business is run, week by week.
6. Growth opportunities you're leaving for the buyer (honest upside, not hype).

## Deal structure & negotiation
- **All-cash** — cleanest, fastest; expect a slight discount for the certainty.
- **Earnout** — part of price paid on the business hitting future targets; bridges a valuation gap
  but ties you in — cap the term and keep targets in your control.
- **Seller financing** — buyer pays over time; widens the buyer pool and can lift total price, but
  you carry default risk — secure it and vet the buyer.

Negotiation tactics: anchor with the asking price justified by comps; sell the *story and SOP*, not
just the spreadsheet; create mild competition (multiple interested buyers) rather than negotiating
1:1; and be willing to walk — the next asset is always coming.
